Abstract

Cross-embodiment dexterous grasp generation remains challenging because robotic hands differ substantially in geometry, topology, and kinematics. Sharing models or aligning hand morphology alone does not ensure consistent functional semantics for grasp interactions. We introduce FIRA-Grasp, which uses Functional Interaction Representation Alignment to align corresponding hand regions and their local interaction frames, giving hand–object and intra-hand relations consistent semantics across embodiments. A node–edge diffusion model predicts the spatial layout of these regions, and fixed inverse kinematics converts the layout into target-hand joint configurations. This separates embodiment-independent grasp generation from embodiment-specific kinematic realization. Adaptation requires only the geometric and kinematic model of a new hand and a one-time lightweight manual functional annotation, without target-hand grasp data, fine-tuning, or learned retargeting. We train FIRA-Grasp on grasps of 48 objects from three embodiments in the filtered CMapDataset. On 10 held-out objects, it achieves average success rates of 92.40% on the three seen embodiments and 74.02% on four unseen embodiments. These results support functional interaction alignment for zero-shot cross-embodiment grasp generation.
